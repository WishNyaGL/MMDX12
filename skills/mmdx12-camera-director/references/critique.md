# Critique

The review is where the film is made. Look at the contact sheets first, then answer these questions in writing. Be
specific: name the frame, name the tile, say what is wrong. "Looks good" is not a critique.

## The rubric

**Composition**
- Is there a clear subject in every shot, and does the frame lead the eye to it?
- Is the headroom right? Too much empty air above the head reads as a mistake; too little crops the head.
- Is there lead room in the direction the performer faces or moves?
- Do background lines (stage edges, beams, columns, other members) cut through the head or grow out of it?
- Is the frame balanced, or is it balanced on purpose?

**Story and singer**
- While a singer sings, are they visible, and is the camera on them or motivated to be elsewhere?
- Does the edit respect who owns each line (solo, call-and-response, unison)?
- Are the signature moments actually on the moments you placed them on?
- Did you miss a gesture the dance offered — a hand, a turn, a look?

**Rhythm and energy**
- Does the cut rate follow the song's curve, or is it flat?
- Is there at least one place to breathe, and one long take that earns its length?
- Do the cuts land on breaths and phrase starts rather than mid-word?
- Does the energy of the movement match the energy of the music at each point?

**Continuity**
- Do consecutive shots differ enough to avoid a jump cut (size, angle or move)?
- Is screen direction preserved? Does the camera cross the axis only when intended?
- Is the 180-degree rule kept within a scene?
- Do the yaws alternate left/right without reason (the wiper)?

**Treatment fidelity**
- Could a stranger watch the film and describe your logline, point of view and motif?
- Did you break a restraint rule?
- Would this film work for a different song? If yes, it is not specific enough.

## The failures and how to fix them

| What you see | What it means | Fix |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| The head is clipped at the top | headroom too tight | widen one size or lower `pitch_deg`; re-solve the shot |
| A beam or column grows out of the head | background line | shift yaw by 10-20 degrees, or change height |
| The performer has nowhere to look | no lead room | add `lead` 0.06-0.15 in the facing direction |
| The singer is off-screen during their line | coverage ignored the vocal | re-cut that range to the singer, or make the wide motivated |
| Every shot is the same size | monotony | plan a size change of at least two steps between neighbours |
| Every cut is on the downbeat | template rhythm | move cuts to phrase starts and breaths; leave a bar uncut |
| The camera never stops | no stillness | make the next shot static and let the dance read |
| Left/right/left yaw | wiper | keep yaws on one side of the subject across the scene |
| Two adjacent shots look the same | jump cut | change size, angle by 30 degrees, or the move |
| A shot has no subject | dead shot | cut it, or give it a reason (a held empty frame is a choice) |
| The signature move reads as a pan | movement not composed | hand-author the waypoints; vary speed and framing along the path |
| The film is beautiful but generic | no point of view | rewrite the treatment, not the shots |
| The energy is flat in the chorus | cut rate or movement too small | accelerate cuts and increase move amplitude there only |

## Example critique of a weak draft

Draft: a 24-shot pass on a 3:10 group song, every shot a medium, cuts on every downbeat, camera yaw alternating
+20/-20, the treatment said "a fan in the front row".

> The film is competent and anonymous. Every shot is a medium from the front, so the formation never reads and the
> five members become a texture. The yaw alternates on every cut, which makes the camera feel like a metronome, not a
> fan. The cut on every downbeat flattens the song: the verse and the chorus have the same pulse. At 1:44 the
> call-and-response is shot as one continuous medium with both singers, so the conversation disappears. The drop at
> 0:58 is a cut to another medium, not an event. The motif — the others reacting — is never shot. Nothing is broken
> technically; no head is cut. It is just not directed.

Revision notes:

- Rewrote the opening as a wide that establishes the five and the room, then a full shot tracking the first step
  into the drop. The drop is now a change of distance and direction, not another medium.
- Set all verse yaws to +15..+25 (one side); the wiper is gone. The chorus keeps that side and changes size instead.
- Moved verse cuts to phrase starts and left one 4-bar static medium on the second verse. The chorus now accelerates.
- Re-shot 1:44 as two mcus on opposite sides of the axis, cut on each phrase. It reads as a duel.
- Added the reaction cut at 1:12 (the member not singing, laughing) and at 2:19. The motif is now visible.
- Second pass sheet: the formation reads in the wide, the verse breathes, the drop lands. The remaining problem is
  the final chorus, which is still all mediums — flagged for a third pass.

## After the critique

Revise only what the critique found. Do not redraw the whole film in response to one bad tile. Re-render the same
sheet, put the before and after next to each other, and write two or three lines of director's notes: what changed,
why, and what is still unresolved.
