# Treatment template

Fill this in before you plan a shot. Write in sentences you would say out loud. Short and opinionated beats long and
safe.

## The form

```
Song / artist:
What it is about (3-5 lines):
Emotional arc, and where it peaks:

Logline of the visual idea (1-2 sentences):

Point of view — who is the camera?
   (audience member / a friend / a voyeur / the song itself / her own memory / ...)

Camera language:
   locked-off | floating | restless:
   lens policy (what lenses, and when):
   how close we are allowed to get:

Relation to light (what the camera sees, what it hides):

The motif (one thing that repeats and means something):

Signature moments (2-4), each with a frame and a reason:
   1.
   2.
   3.

Restraint — what I will NOT do in this film:
   -
   -
```

## Example A — intimate solo ballad

Song: a slow piano ballad, one singer, no dancers. 3:40.

What it is about: someone singing to a person who has already left the room. The arc is not sadness to joy; it is
denial to admission. It peaks at the last chorus, where the voice stops being careful.

Logline: the camera is the absent person, and it cannot make itself leave.

Point of view: the person being sung to, standing a metre away, unable to answer. The camera never moves quickly
because that person is not going anywhere.

Camera language: mostly locked-off, long lenses (14-18 degrees), close. The camera is allowed three moves in the
whole film, all slow pushes. We are never further than a medium shot from her, and after the second verse never
further than a close-up.

Relation to light: a single warm source, low and to one side, so half her face is always in shadow. The room is dark
around her; we never see the walls. On the final chorus the light does not get brighter — it gets closer.

Motif: her hands. Every time the lyric admits something, the frame finds her hands before her face.

Signature moments:
1. 2:31, the last "stay" — a 40-frame slow push from cu to ecu, no cut, ending on her eyes after she closes them.
2. 3:02, the final chorus — the camera arcs 15 degrees around her, the only lateral move in the film, so the room
   feels like it turns.
3. 3:28, the last line — locked-off wide of the empty chair beside her; she is small in frame for the first time.

Restraint: no cuts on the beat; no crane; no shots from behind her; never show the empty room until the last line.

## Example B — high-energy group idol chorus

Song: 5-member dance track, 3:10, four-on-the-floor, big drop at 0:58.

What it is about: five people who have decided tonight is the best night of their lives, and they want you in it.
The arc is anticipation (verse), explosion (drop), joy that keeps finding new gears (final chorus).

Logline: a camera that is one of the crowd, pushed onto the stage and then let loose inside the formation.

Point of view: a fan in the front row who somehow got onto the stage. Handheld energy, but never chaotic — the
camera is excited, not drunk.

Camera language: restless in the chorus, floating in the verse. Wide-to-normal lenses (22-32) so the formation
reads and the room feels big; telephoto (18) only for solo close-ups. The camera is close enough to be hit by a hand.

Relation to light: the camera lives in the beams. Backlight and haze do the work; faces are lit but the frame is
allowed to flare when the camera crosses a spot.

Motif: the camera finds the member who is NOT singing and catches them reacting. The group is a group because of
what the others do during a solo.

Signature moments:
1. 0:58, the drop — hard cut from a wide of the whole formation to a full shot inside the line, camera trucking
   right with the first step. The cut lands on the first kick, and the truck continues the direction of the dance.
2. 1:44, the call-and-response — two mcu shots, hard-cut between the two singers on their phrases, each camera on
   the opposite side of the axis to make it a duel.
3. 2:37, the final chorus — one 90-frame crane up from full to wide, revealing the whole formation in a star, no
   cuts; the only time we see all five at once in the last minute.

Restraint: no strobe cutting in the verse; no dutch angles; no shot longer than 2 bars in the chorus; never lose the
singer of a solo line.

## What makes a treatment usable

- It names a point of view, so every angle question has an answer.
- It can be broken. If the contact sheets show the idea is not reading, the treatment is what you revise — not the
  shot numbers.
- It has restraint. A film with no "will not" is a pile of options.
